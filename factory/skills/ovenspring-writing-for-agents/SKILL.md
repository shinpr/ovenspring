# Writing for agents

Write so the receiving LLM can act or judge from the supplied context. These principles are the foundation; the relevant role, procedure, or skill supplies the artifact's particular content and format.

## Reader and language

Identify who consumes the text and what they use it for. Write agent-facing content in English, including instructions generated from a human's request in another language. Human-facing documents and messages follow the project's language rules. Keep source quotations, identifiers, and user-facing text in their required form; explain their significance in the surrounding text.

## Content

- **Preserve meaning and authority.** Pass existing decisions, designs, and constraints by path and relevant section rather than rewriting them as instructions. A handoff identifies the assigned result and its governing sources; new choices and their reasons belong in the artifact the author owns. Distinguish those choices from observations, inferences, examples, and open questions. An interpretation remains accountable to its source.
- **Make the next action judgeable.** State the purpose, the action or judgment needed, the inputs it depends on, and what observable result answers it. For a judgment, give the criterion and evidence; for execution, give the required outcome and how it can be verified. Include only the parts the current recipient needs.
- **Clarify where correctness depends on it.** Resolve ambiguity when plausible readings change the outcome, scope, usability, or verification. Where the source leaves a point open, use the least restrictive sufficient criterion. Leave reversible choices to the recipient where the purpose, evidence, and boundaries already support a decision; a preference can guide judgment without becoming a fixed procedure.
- **State the desired behavior.** Lead with the action or allowed state. Retain an explicit prohibition only for an irreversible boundary whose meaning would otherwise be lost, paired with the allowed alternative and the condition authorizing a change.
- **Keep context sufficient and compact.** Retain information that changes a decision, action, or verification. Put related conditions together. When the recipient cannot access a source, supply the necessary passage verbatim with its provenance; keep interpretation separate. Use examples only for a mapping or exception the rule alone leaves unclear.
- **Expose dependencies and uncertainty.** Order steps where a later action depends on an earlier result. For missing or contradictory information that prevents the assigned result, name its effect and the evidence or decision needed to proceed. Leave unaffected work available under the settled conditions and the role's authority.
- **Bound obligations and output.** Require only work and output the outcome, a real consumer, or necessary proof needs. Treat suggestions and discovered possibilities as candidates. Keep reuse, no change, and evidence-backed decline available. Follow the artifact's existing format; exact serialization belongs at a machine-parsed boundary.

Before handing over, read the text against its sources as the recipient: can it determine the next action or judgment, its basis, and when its responsibility ends? Correct gaps that change that result and remove additions that merely prescribe a route. This check improves the text itself; its output is the requested artifact.

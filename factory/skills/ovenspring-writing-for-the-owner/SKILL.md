# Writing for the owner

The owner owns the product and acts on the factory's work only through the messages the factory sends them: deciding, agreeing, answering, or learning what changed. Everything that reaches them is written for that act, so they can act from it alone, in the order they act, and their reply comes quickly and means what the factory takes it to mean.

Before writing, settle what you ask of them (or that nothing is asked), what changed for them, and the one thing they should keep if they remember nothing else.

## Structure

Write a question, proposal, or review for the owner in three parts, in this order:

1. **Conclusion.** One or two sentences: what happened or what you propose, and what you ask of them.
2. **Points.** Each thing the owner decides, answers, or should know, as a numbered line they can act on from that line alone: a question they can answer, a proposal they can agree to, a change they should know.
3. **Details.** Under headings, what each point needs, numbered as the points: the options, the evidence and its sources, your reading.

The owner sees the text before the first heading, the conclusion and the points, first; the details follow it, opened when a point needs them. So the first heading starts the details, and each point is understandable without them.

A conversation reply is posted as its text. Give the answer or proposal and what the owner needs to act on it; use headings only when they help reading.

## Content

- Keep a sentence only when it changes what the owner decides or understands. State each claim as exactly what was shown, so it needs no limit after it. Everything else stays on the board: how the work was checked, commit ids, tool versions, the items filed, and what was not claimed or not changed.
- Choose the form by what the owner must grasp: a list for parallel points, a table to compare items on the same attributes.
- Where the owner's own words decide something, quote them.
- A question asking the owner's view starts from the problem, why it matters, and what you think causes it.

## Words

- Write plainly. Say what an internal id, or a term the factory coined, stands for (key-result numbers, review rounds, item and step names) instead of using it, unless the owner used it first.
- A conversation reply is a few sentences. The conclusion and the points fit in about one screen.
